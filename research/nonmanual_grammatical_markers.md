## Facial Information: Non-Manual Markers (NMMs)

Non-manual markers (NMMs) are components of signing that communicate grammatical information in sign language. They involve facial expressions, eye movements, head movements, and body posture rather than hand gestures alone.

### 1. Manual vs. Non-Manual Features

| Feature Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual Features (M)** | Movements and configurations produced using the hands and arms. | Hand shape, hand movement, orientation, and location |
| **Non-Manual Features (NM)** | Visual signals produced without relying on hand gestures. | Eyebrow movements, eye gaze, facial expressions, mouth shapes, head tilting or shaking, body posture, and body movement |

### 2. Functions of Non-Manual Markers

- **Expressing grammatical meaning:** Communicate sentence structure, questions, negation, emphasis, and other grammatical features.
- **Visual equivalent of vocal prosody:** Function similarly to vocal intonation and pitch in spoken languages. For example, eyebrow movements can mark questions or topic shifts.
- **Modifying meaning:** Facial and mouth movements, such as puffing cheeks, wrinkling the nose, or changing lip shapes, can modify manual signs to express degree, intensity, scale, or manner.

### 3. Advantages of Non-Manual Features

- **Improve recognition:** Provide additional visual information to support sign recognition.
- **Prevent ambiguity:** Help distinguish between different grammatical interpretations.
- **Provide context:** Add linguistic information that may not be conveyed through hand gestures alone.
- **Distinguish similar signs:** Help differentiate signs that have identical or similar manual components.

### 4. Non-Manual Markers in Korean Sign Language (KSL)

In KSL, non-manual markers function as part of the language's grammar rather than simply expressing emotions.

KSL uses a combination of spatial arrangement, linguistic context, and non-manual expressions to communicate grammatical relationships. These features contribute to sentence structure and interpretation, alongside manual signs and other grammatical mechanisms.

### 5. Types of Non-Manual Grammatical Markers

The following examples describe common patterns across sign languages. Their specific forms and grammatical functions may differ in KSL.

#### A. Questions (Interrogative Markers)

Facial expressions can help distinguish different types of questions.

**1. Yes/No Questions**

Common cross-linguistic patterns:
- Raised eyebrows.
- Forward head movement.
- Widened eyes.

Example: "Are you a student?"

**2. WH-Questions**

Common cross-linguistic patterns:
- Furrowed eyebrows.
- Forward head movement.
- Particular eye movements.

Example: "Where do you live?"

#### B. Negation Markers

Negation communicates that something is not true, does not happen, or does not exist.

Common non-manual features include:
- Head shaking from side to side.
- Furrowed eyebrows.
- Tightened lips.
- Changes in mouth shape.
- Head tilting.

Example:
- "I want to go."
- "I don't want to go."

Manual signs may include an explicit negative sign, while head and facial movements can reinforce or contribute to the expression of negation.

#### C. Topic Markers

A topic is the person or thing that a sentence is about.

Possible non-manual features include:
- Raised eyebrows.
- Slight backward head movement.
- Changes in facial expression over the topic phrase.

Example: "MY FRIEND, I MET YESTERDAY."

The signer can use non-manual signals to distinguish the topic ("MY FRIEND") from the rest of the sentence.

#### D. Conditional Markers

Conditional sentences express an *if–then* relationship.

Example: "If it rains tomorrow, I will stay home."

Non-manual features reported in sign languages include:
- Raised eyebrows.
- Widened eyes.
- Forward head movement.

These signals can extend over the conditional clause rather than occurring only with an individual sign.

#### E. Emphasis and Focus Markers

These markers highlight important information in a sentence.

Example: "I WANT THE RED ONE."

The signer may emphasize **RED** through:
- Increased eye contact.
- Eyebrow movements.
- Wider eyes.
- Changes in signing speed or sign duration.
- Head movements.

### 6. Important Consideration for KSL

The examples of questions, negation, topic, conditional, and emphasis markers above are informed by broader sign-language research.

Different sign languages do not necessarily use identical facial expressions for the same grammatical function. Therefore, KSL-specific grammatical patterns should be verified through linguistic research and native or proficient KSL signers rather than directly assuming that patterns from other sign languages apply to KSL.

### 7. Suggested NMM Feature Extraction

| Feature Category | Features to Detect | Potential Information |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrows | Raised, lowered, furrowed | Questions, focus, emphasis |
| Eyes | Wide, squinted, gaze direction | Focus and grammatical distinctions |
| Mouth | Mouth opening, lip rounding, mouthing | Additional linguistic information |
| Head | Nod, shake, tilt, forward/backward | Questions, negation, emphasis |
| Upper Body | Leaning, bending, posture | Discourse and prosodic information |
